Most trading agents can tell you what they bought.

Very few can tell you what it costs them if it goes against them — before they buy it.

Give an autonomous agent a brokerage account and it will place trades. That part is solved. The unanswered question is what happens when it is wrong.

The problem02

The flat floors are the claim.

keeps the credit while price stays between the short strikes — $3,727 collected loss cannot deepen past the floor — $26,573 reserved

An iron condor is four legs, which is exactly Alpaca’s --legs limit. Only one side can ever be breached, so the maximum loss is width minus credit — known before the order exists.

The structure03

The proof and the order are the same request.

alpaca order submit --order-class mleg --qty 5
  --type limit --limit-price 0.53
  --client-order-id vig-3f9a1c22
  --legs [ 4 legs ]        --dry-run   ← renders it
  --legs [ 4 legs ]                     ← then sends it

Execution runs entirely through the Alpaca CLI. Every order is submitted twice: once rendered without sending, then again as the identical command. Each structure carries its receipt, and the receipt is the command.

The mechanism04

Eight gates. Every one can say no.

  • 01illiquidany leg’s bid/ask wider than $0.15
  • 02thin-creditcredit under 10% of width
  • 03position-too-largeone structure risking more than 5% of equity
  • 04bucket-fullone underlying and expiry past 6% of equity
  • 05book-fulltotal reserved past the 25% ceiling
  • 06no-wingthe protective strike does not exist
  • 07invertedshort strikes crossed; loss no longer bounded
  • 08regimethe newswire says today is a bad day to be short premium

The result06

What this does not claim.

Paper trading, and roughly two and a half sessions of it. That is not a sample size from which an edge can be inferred, and none is claimed. Selling premium wins often and loses occasionally larger; that skew is real, and the ceilings bound what it can cost rather than pretending it away.

What is checkable is narrower. Every position is covered before it exists.
